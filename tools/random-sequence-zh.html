Random Sequence Generator

Generate random DNA or RNA sequences, design barcode sets with sequence-distance constraints, and independently validate barcode sets.

Sequence filters

Barcode distance constraints

Distance constraints are applied between different selected sequences. A sequence is never compared against itself or its own reverse complement. All generated sequences are unique regardless of whether distance constraints are enabled.
Pairwise distance constraints can substantially reduce the number of feasible barcodes. The generator uses bounded candidate sampling; failure to reach the requested number does not prove that no valid barcode set exists.

2. Barcode Set Validator

Independently evaluate an existing barcode set. Paste sequences in FASTA format or enter one sequence per line.

The validator accepts unambiguous DNA (A/C/G/T) or RNA (A/C/G/U) sequences. IUPAC ambiguity codes are not accepted here because Hamming and reverse-complement distances require unambiguous bases for the calculations implemented in this tool.
FASTA headers beginning with > are supported. For plain text, each non-empty line is treated as one sequence. Whitespace within sequence lines is ignored.

Pairwise distance validation

To prevent excessive browser computation, the validator limits the number of pairwise comparisons to 2,000,000. The limit is checked before pairwise calculations begin. If the input exceeds this limit, reduce the number of sequences.

Distance definitions

Hamming distance: the number of nucleotide positions that differ between two sequences of equal length.

Reverse-complement distance: the Hamming distance between one sequence and the reverse complement of another sequence. The comparison is performed between distinct barcode records.

Important limitations

GC content and homopolymer filtering are sequence-level screening criteria. Hamming and reverse-complement distances are computational measures of sequence dissimilarity. These metrics do not independently predict PCR cross-amplification, secondary structure, barcode misassignment, sequencing error rates, or experimental performance.

The generated barcode sets should therefore be treated as in-silico candidates and subjected to additional experimental and application-specific evaluation before use.
